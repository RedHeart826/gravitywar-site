Privacy Policy

Effective November 17, 2026 · Applies to the Gravity War app for iPhone and iPad

In short: we collect no data. There are no accounts and no analytics, and your games and settings stay on your device. The game is free and shows ads from Google AdMob. Nothing ad-related starts until the first moment an ad could appear; from then on, Google may collect some data, described below. We never track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Who we are

Gravity War is made by RedHeart. You can reach us at techsupport24ca@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

The app saves your settings, your campaign progress, your statistics, and any match in progress, so you can pick up where you left off. If you play with friends, on one device or two, their names and your results against them are saved the same way, for your records. This information is stored on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own iCloud account (below). We cannot see it.

Saving progress in iCloud

If you are signed in to iCloud, your campaign progress, statistics and achievements are also saved in your own iCloud account, so they follow you to your other devices and come back after you reinstall the game. Only your devices signed in to your Apple Account can read them; we cannot. Each install keeps its own statistics there under a random number that is not linked to you or your device. You can turn this off in Settings > Progress > iCloud sync; turning it off deletes nothing.

No internet requests of our own

The game itself sends nothing to us. It runs no server of its own: saving to iCloud and playing online go through Apple (below). New campaign levels come with app updates, and the game plays fully offline (without a connection, no ads appear). The only internet requests come from Google's ad software, below. There are no in-app purchases.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Gravity War is free because it shows ads: a short full-screen ad now and then after a won campaign level or a finished Skirmish, pass-and-play or live online match (never during a match, never in a turn-based online match, and never when you play someone nearby), and optional videos you choose to watch for help in the campaign. Ads start only at the first moment one could appear: your first match from campaign chapter 3 on, the first time help is offered, or your first Skirmish or pass-and-play match. Playing online doesn't start it. Until then the ad software doesn't run. From then on it starts with the app.

To show and measure ads, Google's Mobile Ads SDK may collect:

  • your IP address, used to estimate your general location (country or region);
  • identifiers limited to this app on your device (the game never asks to track you, so your advertising identifier is not shared);
  • which ads you saw and how you interacted with them, for example video views and taps;
  • diagnostic data such as crash logs and performance data.

Google uses this data to show ads, measure them, prevent fraud and improve its services. We do not receive it. The game does not ask to track you, so ads are not personalized based on your activity in other companies' apps or websites. How Google handles this data: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

Your choices. Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA, the UK and some US states), Google's consent form asks before any ad, and you can change your choice later in Settings > Privacy > Ad choices. Ads are limited to general-audience content.

Playing with someone nearby

Two people can play on two devices. One player hosts a match and the other joins it with the code shown on the host's screen. To find each other, the devices use your local network or a direct Wi-Fi link between them, which is why iOS asks for permission to find devices on your local network.

During the match the two devices exchange only what the game needs: the players' names, the match settings, the levels, which weapons each player may use, and each move. This information goes directly between the two devices, encrypted with a key made from the code. It never passes through a server, and we never receive it. Declining the permission only turns off playing on two devices.

Playing online

Online play uses Apple's Game Center. The game signs you in to Game Center only when you open Online. Game Center finds your opponent, a friend you invite or anyone playing, and shows them your Game Center name. In a live match, the same information as in a nearby match (names, match settings, levels and moves) goes between the two devices through Apple's servers. In a turn-based match, Game Center keeps the state of the match (its settings, the current level, the shots and the round results) on Apple's servers until the match is removed, so each player can take their turn when they like. There is no chat. We never receive any of it. How Apple handles Game Center data: Apple's Privacy Policy.

What we do not do

  • We do not collect, sell, or share personal information. Google's ad software collects the data described above for Google, not for us.
  • We do not use analytics or tracking services, and we do not track you across apps or websites. No ads appear while you play someone nearby.
  • We do not require an account. Online play uses your Game Center sign-in, and only if you choose to play online.

Crash reports

If you choose to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may provide us with anonymous crash reports and usage statistics. This is controlled by you in iOS Settings and is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

Children

We collect no personal information from anyone, including children. Gravity War is not directed at children under 13; its ads are limited to general-audience content and are not personalized.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change, for example when a new feature is added, we will update this page and its effective date before the change reaches the app.
